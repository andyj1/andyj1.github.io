    M1 · Malmö old town & beach promenade

    Mapped loop 9.4 km · easy · 60–90 min riding · allow 2–3 hours with stops. Show M1 on the map

    1. Start at Malmö C / Travelshop; Stortorget, S:t Petri and Lilla torg are a short old-town detour. Walk the bike on pedestrian areas.
    2. Continue to Malmöhus, the castle parks and Fiskehoddorna fish huts; add a daytime food stop if open.
    3. Ride to Ribersborg beach promenade, the highlight in your Komoot link. Park for the Kallbadhus pier; a sauna visit needs extra time.
    4. Follow the coast toward Scaniaparken / Västra Hamnen, view Turning Torso from outside, then return to Malmö C via Malmö Saluhall.

    Rental: Travelshop for a continuous ride; Malmö by bike for shorter legs if its return rules suit you. Shorten: skip Västra Hamnen and return after Ribersborg. In wind or rain, walk the castle parks instead.

    M2 · Malmö coast to the Øresund Bridge viewpoint

    Mapped return ride 22 km · moderate distance, exposed coast · 1½–2½ hours riding · allow 3–4 hours. Show M2 on the map

    1. Start at Malmö C and ride via Ribersborg's seaside paths.
    2. Continue through Limhamn toward Sibbarp; use signed cycle streets around the harbour.
    3. Make Luftkastellet / Lernacken your bridge-photo turnaround, then retrace the coast to Malmö C.

    Rental: a day-hire city bike or e-bike from Travelshop. This adapts the coastal bridge-view idea in the MyRealTrip trip and traveller reviews. Turn back at Limhamn if the headwind is tiring; keep off the motorway and bridge approach.

    Conference fit: only a free half-day after checking the programme. Skip it on presentation mornings and on departure day.

    Before riding

    • Keep right, follow bicycle signals and signal turns / stops. Pull completely out of the lane for photos or navigation.
    • Walk the bike in pedestrian areas, crowded squares and any park or attraction that prohibits cycling. Bring a helmet, lights, lock and rain layer.

    Dricks / Tipping culture

    🇸🇪 Sverige / Sweden & 🇩🇰 Danmark / Denmark

    Both countries include fair wages in menu prices. Tipping is appreciated but never obligatory — unlike the US. Round up or add 5–10% only for exceptional service.

    ☕ Caféer / Cafés

    • No tip expected. Pay at counter; say tack (thanks). Round up öre (cents) if paying cash — e.g. SEK 47 → 50.
    • Card/contactless is default everywhere (Swish in Sweden, MobilePay in Denmark).

    🍽 Restauranger / Restaurants

    • Sweden: Service included. Optional 5–10% for excellent table service at nicer places (Vollmers, Bastard). Not expected at food halls, IKEA, or counter service.
    • Denmark: Similarly — service included. Round up DKK 10–50 or ~10% at fine dining. Casual lunch: zero tip is normal.
    • Check bill for serviceavgift (service charge) — rare but if present, no extra tip needed.

    🍺 Barer / Bars & pubar (pubs)

    • Round up to nearest SEK/DKK 10 at craft-beer bars (Spill). Not required at self-service.

    🚕 Taxi & hotel

    • Taxi: Round up 5–10%. Uber/Bolt: optional in-app tip — most locals skip it.
    • Hotel: SEK/DKK 20–50 for porter; leave nothing for daily housekeeping unless exceptional (not customary in Scandinavia).
